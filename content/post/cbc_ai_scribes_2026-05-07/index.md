Dr. Nunez joined CBC Radio's **On the Coast** host Gloria Macarenko to discuss the growing use of AI scribes in clinical settings, including the risks and benefits of using these tools to record and document patient visits.

You can listen to the full 7-minute segment on the [CBC website](https://www.cbc.ca/listen/live-radio/1-46-on-the-coast/clip/16213443-bc-physicians-use-ai-scribes-document-patient-visits).

This segment relates to our recent paper, [**Perspective: Listening to Users when Auditing Medical AI Scribes**](https://koenecke.infosci.cornell.edu/files/ML4H_Koenecke2025.pdf) (Koenecke, Nunez, Rameau, Chen), which argues for more principled audits of medical AI scribes. While these tools offer real efficiency benefits, they pose risks through transcription errors and hallucinations that fall disproportionately on patients with speech disorders or psychiatric illnesses. We propose a framework involving diverse speech datasets, richer evaluation metrics beyond word error rates, and human-centered design research.
